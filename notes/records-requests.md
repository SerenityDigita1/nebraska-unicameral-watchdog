# Records requests — shock gloves story

Two documents are marked pending on `/shock-gloves`. This is what to ask for and who from.

Nebraska Public Records Law is **Neb. Rev. Stat. § 84-712 et seq.** Agencies must respond
within **four business days**. They can charge for copies but not for the act of looking.

---

## 1. The 18-page council submission — try the City Clerk first

**Check before requesting.** Omaha City Council agendas and their attachments are usually
posted online. The item is on the **June 3, 2025** regular meeting agenda, a purchase of 40
pairs of gloves for just under $66,000, requested by the Police Department. If the packet is
posted, the 18 pages are likely attached to the agenda item and you can just download them.

Start at **cityofomaha.org/council** → agendas/minutes → June 3, 2025.

If it is not online, request it:

> **To:** Omaha City Clerk
> **Re:** Public records request — Neb. Rev. Stat. § 84-712
>
> I am requesting a copy of the following public records:
>
> 1. The full agenda item and all supporting materials submitted to the Omaha City Council for
>    the June 3, 2025 regular meeting relating to the purchase of Compliant Technologies
>    G.L.O.V.E. devices by the Omaha Police Department, including the approximately 18-page
>    submission prepared by the Police Department.
> 2. Any purchase order, contract, or invoice associated with that purchase.
>
> If any portion is withheld, please cite the specific statutory exemption relied upon and
> release the remainder. I would prefer electronic copies. Please advise of any fees before
> incurring them.

## 2. The use-of-force policy — Omaha Police Department

This is the one that matters most. The whole story rests on whether policy authorizes use
against **passive resistance**, and that should be quoted from the directive itself.

**Omaha Police Department, Attn: Records Manager**
505 S. 15th Street, Omaha, NE 68102
Walk-in: Central Headquarters front desk, 8am–4pm daily
[Online request form](https://police.cityofomaha.org/services/citizen-services/omaha-police-department-public-records-requests)

> **Re:** Public records request — Neb. Rev. Stat. § 84-712
>
> I am requesting copies of the following:
>
> 1. All current Omaha Police Department policies, directives, general orders, or standard
>    operating procedures governing the use of the Compliant Technologies G.L.O.V.E. device,
>    including any provisions addressing use against subjects who are passively resisting.
> 2. The written use-of-force continuum or force matrix in effect from May 2025 to the present,
>    showing where the device is placed relative to other force options.
> 3. Training materials, lesson plans, or certification requirements provided to school
>    resource officers for the device.
> 4. Any policy specific to use of the device on persons under 18, or in a school setting.
> 5. The total number of deployments of the device by any officer since acquisition, broken
>    out by whether the subject was a minor.
>
> If any portion is withheld, please cite the specific statutory exemption and release the
> remainder.

**What to expect.** Items 1 through 4 are ordinary policy documents and should be released.
Item 5 is a count rather than a record about any individual, so it is harder to refuse.

**What they will likely refuse:** the two individual use-of-force reports. Those involve
identifiable students and will be denied or redacted to uselessness under student privacy and
juvenile record provisions. Do not build the story on getting them — that is why the piece is
framed around the policy rather than the incidents.

## 3. LB1039 — no request needed

Committee records are published. Look for:

- The **committee statement** for LB1039 (Education Committee), which records the vote to
  advance or hold
- The **hearing transcript** for the February 9, 2026 hearing, which shows who testified for
  and against
- The bill's **action history**, which shows whether it was voted down in committee or simply
  never advanced before the session ended

Start at **nebraskalegislature.gov** → Bills → search LB1039, 109th Legislature.

**Why this distinction matters.** In Nebraska, "indefinitely postponed" is frequently the
automatic end-of-session disposition for a bill still sitting in committee — not a recorded
vote against it. The article currently says so and flags it as unverified. If the committee
actually voted it down, that is a much stronger fact and names names. If it just died, the
story is neglect rather than rejection. Both are worth writing; they are not the same sentence.

## 4. Millard Public Schools — what to actually ask

John is a Millard constituent, which is the strongest position to ask from. Two separate
tracks, and they are not interchangeable.

### Track A: the records request (to the district, not the board)

The district holds the agreement that governs what an officer may do in its buildings. This
is the document to get, and it does not depend on Omaha Police releasing anything.

> **Re:** Public records request — Neb. Rev. Stat. § 84-712
>
> I am requesting copies of:
>
> 1. The current school resource officer agreement, memorandum of understanding, or contract
>    between Millard Public Schools and any law enforcement agency providing officers to
>    district buildings, including all amendments.
> 2. Any district policy, administrative regulation, or guidance addressing use of force by
>    school resource officers or security personnel on district property.
> 3. Any communication between the district and Omaha Police Department, from January 1, 2025
>    to the present, referencing the Compliant Technologies G.L.O.V.E. device, shock gloves, or
>    electronic control devices carried by school resource officers.
> 4. Any district policy governing notification of a parent or guardian when force is used on
>    a student by a school resource officer.

Item 3 is the one that establishes **when Millard knew**. Item 4 matters because if the
device is ever used on a Millard student, the answer to "who gets told, and how fast" should
already exist — and if it does not, that is itself the finding.

### Track B: the questions to the board

Ask things that have dates and documents in them. "What is the district's position" invites a
statement about student safety and commits nobody to anything.

1. **When did the district first learn** that officers assigned to Millard buildings were
   carrying these devices — before or after the August 13, 2026 news reports?
2. **Does the SRO agreement give Millard any say** over what equipment officers carry in
   district buildings? Omaha Public Schools asked for removal and Omaha Police agreed, so the
   answer appears to be yes. **Has Millard asked?**
3. The district said it plans to learn more from Omaha Police. **Has that meeting happened,
   who attended, and what was the outcome?**
4. **When will the board take a position**, and will it be on a public agenda?
5. **If the device is used on a Millard student, who is notified and within what time?**

Question 2 is the sharp one. There is now a live precedent inside the same city, with the same
police department: a district asked, and the department complied. That converts "we are
learning more" from a reasonable posture into a choice.

### Which venue

Emailing individual board members gets you a reply. **Public comment at a board meeting gets
you a reply on the record**, in the minutes, where it can be quoted and followed up. If the
goal is a publishable answer rather than a private reassurance, use the meeting.

Either way, note the date asked. "The district did not respond by publication" is only usable
if the request is documented.
