Nebraska voters decided four statewide measures on November 3, 2026: one constitutional amendment sent by the Legislature and three measures put on the ballot by petition. Unofficial returns show Amendment 1 and Initiatives 440 and 441 failing and Initiative 442 passing; see the unofficial results below. This page lays out the ballot wording, what each vote did, who was behind each measure, each side's arguments in its own words, the money reported, and the rules for passing. It takes no position on any measure. **Polls closed at 8 PM CT on Nov. 3; results are unofficial until the canvass.**

## How a measure passes

The rule was the same for both kinds of measure on this ballot. For the three petition measures, Article III, Section 4 of the Nebraska Constitution says a measure passes "when a majority of the votes cast thereon, and not less than thirty-five per cent of the total vote cast at the election at which the same was submitted, are cast in favor thereof" ([Neb. Const. art. III, sec. 4](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/articles.php?article=III-4)). For Amendment 1, sent by the Legislature, Article XVI, Section 1 says it is adopted if "a majority of the electors voting on any such amendment adopt the same," provided the yes votes are "not less than thirty-five per cent of the total votes cast at such election" ([Neb. Const. art. XVI, sec. 1](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/articles.php?article=XVI-1)). In plain terms: more yes than no, and the yes votes must equal at least 35% of everyone who cast a ballot in the election.

## Constitutional Amendment 1: legislative term limits

**Ballot wording.** "A constitutional amendment to change the limit on legislative terms from two consecutive terms to three consecutive terms." It would amend Article III, Section 12 ([LR19CA](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/FloorDocs/109/PDF/Final/LR19CA.pdf)).

**What a vote did,** per the Secretary of State's legal notice ([published notice](https://zeta.creativecirclecdn.com/seward/files/20261021-111001-a95-182327_1.pdf)):

- **FOR:** "will amend the state constitution to increase the term limit for state legislators from two consecutive four-year terms (eight years) to three consecutive four-year terms (12 years)."
- **AGAINST:** "will not amend the Nebraska Constitution, so the current limit of two consecutive four-year terms will remain in effect. State legislators will continue to be ineligible for office in the Legislature for four years after serving two consecutive terms."

**Sponsor.** State Sen. Rob Dover of Norfolk introduced LR19CA with 22 other senators, and the Legislature approved it on May 28, 2025 ([Nebraska Examiner](https://nebraskaexaminer.com/2025/05/28/nebraskans-to-decide-in-2026-whether-to-allow-three-four-year-terms-in-legislature/)). Current limits came from a 2000 petition that passed with 55.8% of the vote, and a 2012 proposal to allow three terms received 35.4% ([Nebraska Examiner voter guide](https://nebraskaexaminer.com/voter-guides/ballot-measures/legislative-term-limits-constitutional-amendment/)).

**Arguments.** The Secretary of State's pamphlet covers only petition measures, so it has no official for and against statements for Amendment 1. The summaries below come from news coverage.

- **Supporters:** Dover argued that in a one-house Legislature, "when a representative is termed out" there is no other chamber to move to, so experience is lost ([Nebraska Examiner](https://nebraskaexaminer.com/2025/05/28/nebraskans-to-decide-in-2026-whether-to-allow-three-four-year-terms-in-legislature/)). He said eight years equals about two and a half years in session, and that new senators are pressured by lobbyists ([Nebraska Examiner](https://nebraskaexaminer.com/2026/10/30/voters-will-decide-whether-to-extend-legislative-term-limits-sponsor-isnt-holding-his-breath/)).
- **Opponents:** Supporters of the current limits argued that they "make way for new blood and fresh ideas by limiting 'career politicians' who have become beholden to special interests and lobbyists" ([Nebraska Public Media](https://nebraskapublicmedia.org/en/news/news-articles/nebraskans-to-decide-whether-to-lengthen-legislative-term-limits/)).

**Money.** No campaign committee for or against had registered with the Nebraska Accountability and Disclosure Commission as of Sept. 17 ([Nebraska Examiner voter guide](https://nebraskaexaminer.com/voter-guides/ballot-measures/legislative-term-limits-constitutional-amendment/)). Dover said supporting groups would not fund a campaign ([Nebraska Examiner, Oct. 30](https://nebraskaexaminer.com/2026/10/30/voters-will-decide-whether-to-extend-legislative-term-limits-sponsor-isnt-holding-his-breath/)).

**Legal challenges.** None found.

## Initiative Measures 440 and 441: online sports betting

The two measures work as a pair. 440 changes the constitution and 441 writes the statute. The wording below is from the Secretary of State's pamphlet ([SOS pamphlet](https://sos.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/doc/elections/2026/2026%20Ballot%20Measures%20Pamphlet.pdf)).

**440 ballot wording.** "Shall the Nebraska Constitution be amended to authorize the enactment of laws allowing persons located within Nebraska to place sports wagers through an Internet-based platform when offered by an authorized gaming operator conducting games of chance within a licensed racetrack enclosure in Nebraska?"

- **FOR:** "will amend the Nebraska Constitution to authorize the enactment of laws to allow persons located within Nebraska to place sports wagers through an Internet-based platform when offered by an authorized gaming operator conducting games of chance within a licensed racetrack enclosure in Nebraska."
- **AGAINST:** "means the Nebraska Constitution will not be amended in this manner."

**441 ballot wording.** "Shall statutes be enacted that: (1) Allow persons located within Nebraska to place sports wagers through an Internet-based platform when offered by an authorized gaming operator conducting games of chance within a licensed racetrack enclosure in Nebraska or the operator's platform provider; and (2) Regulate such sports wagers?"

- **FOR:** "will enact statutes allowing persons located within Nebraska to place sports wagers through an Internet-based platform when offered by an authorized gaming operator conducting games of chance within a licensed racetrack enclosure in Nebraska or the operator's platform provider, and regulating such sports wagers."
- **AGAINST:** "means no such statutes will be enacted."

**Sponsor.** The petition campaign was Tax Relief Nebraska. The main opposing group was Save Nebraska Sports ([Nebraska Examiner, Nov. 2](https://nebraskaexaminer.com/2026/11/02/nebraska-candidates-ballot-measures-exceed-63m-in-fundraising-before-nov-3-election/)).

**Arguments, from the SOS pamphlet** ([SOS pamphlet](https://sos.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/doc/elections/2026/2026%20Ballot%20Measures%20Pamphlet.pdf)):

- **Supporters contended:** legal online wagering would keep in Nebraska money that now goes to illegal, offshore or out-of-state sites, and is "projected to deliver more than $166 million in direct property tax relief" over ten years. For 441 they added that "70 percent of all wagering tax revenue goes directly to Nebraska's Property Tax Credit Cash Fund," with oversight by the Nebraska Racing and Gaming Commission.
- **Opponents contended:** the measures would "turn every phone into a casino, every play into a wager, and every athlete into a potential target," sending money to out-of-state gambling companies. They said online betting "fuels corruption in sports," that "every teenager with a phone will face aggressive advertising," and that families would see "no real property tax relief."

**Money.** Tax Relief Nebraska had raised nearly $22.5 million through Oct. 29, including $10.4 million each from DK Crown Holdings (DraftKings' parent) and FanDuel ([Nebraska Examiner, Nov. 2](https://nebraskaexaminer.com/2026/11/02/nebraska-candidates-ballot-measures-exceed-63m-in-fundraising-before-nov-3-election/)). The Examiner did not report a total for Save Nebraska Sports. A third-party summary of state filings lists $1,025,000 raised through Oct. 31, all from Restore the Good Life Inc. ([Plains Ledger](https://www.plainsledger.org/entity/nadc-10661?year=2026)). *Not confirmed on an NADC or Examiner page.*

**Legal challenges.** None found.

## Initiative Measure 442: girls' sports

**Ballot wording.** "Shall the Nebraska Constitution be amended to provide: (1) Schools must expressly designate each athletic team or sport as one of the following based on biological sex: (a) Males, men, or boys; (b) females, women, or girls; or (c) coed or mixed; and (2) Athletic teams or sports designated for females, women, or girls shall not be open to students of the male sex? The amendment would apply to all athletic teams or sports sponsored by public schools or postsecondary educational institutions, and private schools or educational institutions whose athletic teams or sports compete against public schools or institutions." ([SOS pamphlet](https://sos.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/doc/elections/2026/2026%20Ballot%20Measures%20Pamphlet.pdf))

- **FOR:** amends the constitution to require the sex designations and the limit on female teams described in the question.
- **AGAINST:** "means the Nebraska Constitution will not be amended in this manner."

**Sponsor.** The Fairness for Girls campaign, led by former state Sen. Julie Slama of Dunbar. The opposing committee was Fairness for All ([Nebraska Examiner voter guide](https://nebraskaexaminer.com/voter-guides/ballot-measures/fairness-for-girls-constitutional-amendment/)).

**Arguments, from the SOS pamphlet** ([SOS pamphlet](https://sos.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/doc/elections/2026/2026%20Ballot%20Measures%20Pamphlet.pdf)):

- **Supporters contended:** the measure "protects fairness, opportunities, and safety for Nebraska girls in sports." They cited "physical differences in strength, bone density, and size," and said it protects girls "from losing scholarships, medals, or tournament advancement."
- **Opponents contended:** it "adds unnecessary limits on participation in girls' sports," would make "gender verification constitutionally required for girls and young women" but not boys, and "would actually subject all girls to greater barriers and risk of harm and create a constitutional right to bully kids about their appearance."

**Money.** Fairness for Girls reported raising $3.1 million and spending $3.02 million through July 27, almost all from Restore the Good Life Inc., whose donors are not public. Fairness for All reported almost $16,000 in in-kind contributions ([Nebraska Examiner voter guide](https://nebraskaexaminer.com/voter-guides/ballot-measures/fairness-for-girls-constitutional-amendment/)). A third-party summary of later filings lists $3,252,243 raised and $3,168,570 spent for Fairness for Girls, and $109,352 raised for Fairness for All ([Plains Ledger](https://www.plainsledger.org/search?etype=Ballot+question+committee&year=2026)). *Not confirmed on an NADC or Examiner page.*

**Legal challenges.** In *Ullman v. Evnen*, No. S-26-753, a Lincoln voter argued the petition's object statement was not specific enough. Lancaster County District Judge Lori Maret dismissed the challenge, and on Oct. 16, 2026, the Nebraska Supreme Court affirmed 7-0 in an opinion by Justice Derek Vaughn, holding that state law "does not require an object statement to summarize all major provisions of an initiative" ([opinion](https://www.nebraska.gov/apps-courts-epub/public/viewOpinion?docId=N00013530PUB); [Nebraska Examiner](https://nebraskaexaminer.com/2026/10/16/nebraska-supreme-court-upholds-validity-of-fairness-for-girls-ballot-measure/)). Justice Jason Bergevin did not participate; Court of Appeals Judge PaTricia Freeman sat in his place. The measure stayed on the ballot.
